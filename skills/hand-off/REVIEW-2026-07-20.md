## Verdict

Skill is mature and far past MVP. Real work is now **complexity vs. cognitive load** trade-offs, not "does it work". Recommend keeping all core inventions (Multi-Hop Trust Health, provenance tags, `next_action` branching, atomic writes, scope discovery) — they're the differentiators. Focus optimization on **first-load ergonomics** and **empirical validation** of thresholds.

### P1 · Multi-Hop Trust Health empirical calibration
- `[ ]` Add synthetic scenario tests: construct context.md with known compositions (e.g. 30 inferred + 5 git + 10 untagged; 100% git; 50 inferred single-hop) and assert `_analyze_multihop_health` returns expected verdict
- `[ ]` Open a new **`## Empirical Calibration Log`** section in DECISIONS.md, dated 2026-07-20, listing the current thresholds (`inferred_pct ≥ 40`, `untagged_pct ≥ 50`, `hop_count ≥ 3`, `stale_invariants_count ≥ 5`, `soft_conflicts ≥ 3`) as **hypotheses**
- `[ ]` Instrument: every real invocation of `prepare` that returns `challenge_required` should be recorded (project, hop count, verdict fields, user's actual response) — append to the log
- `[ ]` After N=5 real triggers, review whether thresholds fired too often / too seldom and adjust

**Rationale:** rev-F is the most powerful and least battle-tested part of the skill. The thresholds are educated guesses. If `challenge_required` fires on hop 3 of every project, users will train themselves to click through. If it never fires, the mechanism is dead code. Empirical data closes the loop.

- `[-]` Do NOT change the 5-tag scheme yet
- `[ ]` Instead, add lightweight instrumentation: `_extract_provenance_lines` already parses tags — have `prepare` emit a `tag_frequency` field in its output (per-run, not persistent). Log this alongside Empirical Calibration entries.

## Kept As-Is (explicit non-actions)
